MODULE 01

🏛️ What Trading Actually Is

Before any chart or strategy: what markets are, who's on the other side of your trade, and the honest odds — because a course that skips this part is selling you something.

⚠️ Read This First — The Honest Odds

Regulated brokers in the EU and UK are required to publish the percentage of their retail clients who lose money trading leveraged products. The figure is consistently in the range of 70–80%. That's not a scare tactic — it's a disclosure requirement precisely because so many beginners arrive expecting easy profits.

⚠️
Nobody reliably predicts marketsNot banks, not hedge funds, and definitely not social media gurus selling signals. Anyone promising guaranteed returns, secret loopholes, or "95% win rates" is describing a scam, not a strategy (Module 11 covers how to spot them). What separates surviving traders from blown accounts isn't prediction — it's risk management, which is why it's Module 5 here and not an afterthought.

This course will not make you a profitable trader by itself. It will make you someone who understands the mechanics, protects their capital, practices before risking money, and can't be easily fooled — which is the only honest starting point there is.

🏛️ What a Market Is

A market is just a venue where buyers and sellers meet and prices are set by supply and demand. When you buy, someone else is selling to you — usually a professional or an institution. That's worth internalizing: every trade has a counterparty who thinks the opposite of you, and they're often better resourced.

Forex (FX)Currencies, in pairsLargest market on earth (~$7 trillion/day), open 24/5, decentralized

💡
An honest framingFor most people, most of the time, boring long-term investing beats active trading. Learn trading because you want to understand markets deeply or genuinely enjoy the craft — not because you've been told it's a shortcut to income.
🧩 Who's in the Market
  • Retail traders — individuals trading their own money. That's you.
  • Institutional traders — banks, hedge funds, pension funds moving enormous size, with better data, faster execution, and full-time teams.
  • Market makers — firms that continuously quote both buy and sell prices, earning the spread (Module 2) in exchange for providing liquidity.
  • Brokers — your access point to the market. They execute your orders and, importantly, make money whether you win or lose (Module 11).

MODULE 02

💱 Forex Fundamentals

Currency pairs, pips, lots, spreads, and leverage — the vocabulary and math every forex quote is built on.

💱 Currencies Trade in Pairs

You never buy "the euro" alone — you buy one currency with another. EUR/USD = 1.0850 means 1 euro costs 1.0850 US dollars. The first currency is the base, the second is the quote.

Pair TypeExamplesCharacter
MajorsEUR/USD, USD/JPY, GBP/USDMost liquid, tightest spreads — where beginners should stay
CrossesEUR/GBP, AUD/JPYNo USD; wider spreads
ExoticsUSD/TRY, USD/ZARThin liquidity, huge spreads, violent moves — avoid as a beginner

Buying EUR/USD ("going long") profits if the euro strengthens against the dollar. Selling it ("going short") profits if the euro weakens. Being able to profit in both directions is a defining feature of forex.

🔢 Pips and Lots — The Units

A pip is the standard unit of price movement — for most pairs, the 4th decimal place. EUR/USD moving from 1.0850 to 1.0851 is a 1-pip move. (JPY pairs use the 2nd decimal instead.)

Lot SizeUnits of Base CurrencyApprox. Value per Pip (EUR/USD)
Standard lot100,000~$10
Mini lot (0.1)10,000~$1
Micro lot (0.01)1,000~$0.10
💡
Position size math you'll use constantlyProfit or loss = pips moved × pip value × lots. A 50-pip move on 0.1 lots of EUR/USD ≈ 50 × $1 = $50. This one formula is the backbone of the position-sizing rules in Module 5.
↔️ The Spread — Your Cost of Entry

Brokers quote two prices: the bid (what you can sell at) and the ask (what you can buy at). The gap between them is the spread — you pay it on every single trade, which means every position starts slightly in the red. Tight spreads on majors (often under 1 pip) are exactly why beginners should stay there.

⚡ Leverage — The Reason Most Accounts Die

Leverage lets you control a position far larger than your deposit. At 30:1, a $1,000 account can control $30,000 of currency. That multiplies profits and losses identically.

$1,000 account, 30:1 leverage, full size on EUR/USD (0.3 lots ≈ $3/pip) Price moves +100 pips in your favor → +$300 (+30% of account) Price moves -100 pips against you → -$300 (-30% of account) Three of those losses in a row and the account is nearly gone. 100-pip daily swings on majors are completely ordinary.
⚠️
Leverage is a loss amplifier, not free moneyRegulators cap retail leverage (30:1 in EU/UK on majors, 50:1 in the US) specifically because higher leverage correlates directly with faster account destruction. A margin call — your broker force-closing positions when losses eat your deposit — is the mechanical end state. Module 5's position-sizing rules exist to keep you permanently far away from it.

EUR/USD moves from 1.0850 to 1.0900 while you hold 0.1 lots long. Approximately what's your profit?
MODULE 03

📊 Stocks, ETFs & Other Instruments

Forex isn't the only market — and for most people it shouldn't be the first one. Here's the wider instrument landscape and where each fits.

🏢 Stocks — Owning a Slice of a Company

A share is fractional ownership. Its price reflects the market's collective estimate of the company's future — earnings, growth, risk. Stockholders can profit two ways: price appreciation, and dividends (a share of profits paid out, typically quarterly).

🧺 ETFs — The Beginner's Best Friend

An ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund) is a single tradeable share that holds a whole basket of assets. An S&P 500 ETF holds all 500 companies at once — instant diversification for the price of one share.

💡
Why professionals recommend index ETFs to beginnersPicking individual winning stocks is hard even for professionals — most actively managed funds underperform the plain index over long periods. A broad, low-fee index ETF sidesteps the stock-picking problem entirely, which is why it's the standard "boring but effective" core of long-term investing.
🧾 The Rest of the Landscape
InstrumentWhat It IsBeginner Suitability
BondsLoans to governments/companies paying fixed interestFine — the stability side of a portfolio
Index funds / ETFsBaskets tracking a whole marketIdeal starting point
OptionsContracts giving the right to buy/sell at a set price by a dateAdvanced — easy to lose 100% of a position; learn much later
FuturesObligation to buy/sell at a set price on a date; leveragedAdvanced — institutional tool first
CFDsLeveraged bets on price without owning the asset (how most retail "forex" is actually traded)This is where those 70–80% loss disclosures come from — extreme caution
CryptoDigital assets, 24/7, very volatileIf at all: small, money you can afford to lose entirely
🗺️ A Sane Order of Operations
  1. Emergency fund first — never trade or invest money you may need within months.
  2. Long-term core — broad index ETFs, boring and consistent.
  3. Then, if genuinely interested, learn trading — on a paper account (Modules 10, 12) with money you could lose entirely once live.

MODULE 04

🎯 How Orders Work

Market, limit, stop — and the two orders that should be attached to every trade you ever place: the stop loss and take profit.

📋 The Core Order Types
OrderWhat It DoesUse When
Market orderExecutes immediately at the best available priceYou want in/out right now and accept small slippage
Limit orderExecutes only at your price or betterYou want to buy lower / sell higher than the current price, and will wait
Stop orderBecomes a market order once price crosses your triggerEntering on momentum, or exiting to cut a loss

Slippage — the gap between the price you expected and the price you actually got — is normal in fast markets, and it's why a "stop" is a trigger, not a guarantee of an exact fill price.

🛡️ Stop Loss & Take Profit — Non-Negotiable

A stop loss (SL) automatically closes your trade at a predefined loss level. A take profit (TP) automatically closes it at a predefined gain. Together they define your trade's outcome range before you enter — while you're calm, not while you're watching money move.

Long EUR/USD @ 1.0850 1.0950 ── Take Profit (+100 pips) ▲ 1.0850 ── Entry ▼ 1.0800 ── Stop Loss (-50 pips) Risk : Reward = 50 : 100 = 1 : 2
⚠️
Trading without a stop loss is how single trades destroy accounts"It'll come back" is the most expensive sentence in trading. Decide your exit before entry, place the SL as part of the order, and never widen it after the fact. The simulator in Module 12 requires an SL on every trade for exactly this reason.
⚖️ Risk:Reward Ratio

The diagram above risks 50 pips to target 100 — a 1:2 risk:reward. At 1:2, you only need to win 34% of trades to break even. That's the arithmetic that makes disciplined traders survivable despite losing more often than winning — and it's set entirely by where you place SL and TP.

MODULE 05

🛡️ Risk Management First

The centerpiece of this course. Strategy determines whether you win a given trade; risk management determines whether you're still around after a losing streak — and losing streaks are guaranteed.

1️⃣ The 1% Rule

Risk no more than 1% of your account on any single trade (2% at the aggressive end). "Risk" means the amount you lose if your stop loss is hit — not your position size.

Why small risk per trade is survival math — 10 consecutive losses: Risking 1% per trade → account down ~9.6% (fully recoverable) Risking 5% per trade → account down ~40% (needs +67% to recover) Risking 10% per trade → account down ~65% (needs +186% to recover) A 10-trade losing streak is not rare bad luck. Over enough trades it's a statistical certainty.
🧮 Position Sizing — The Formula

Position size isn't a feeling; it's derived from three numbers you already know before entering:

Lots = (Account × Risk%) ÷ (Stop distance in pips × pip value per lot) Account $5,000, risking 1% = $50 Stop loss distance: 25 pips Pip value (EUR/USD, per mini lot): $1 Lots = $50 ÷ (25 × $10 per standard lot) = 0.2 lots

Notice the causality: the stop loss location (a chart decision) and the risk budget (an account decision) together determine position size. Beginners do it backwards — pick a size that "feels right," then discover their real risk was 10× intended.

📉 Drawdown & the Asymmetry of Loss
LossGain Needed to Recover
-10%+11%
-25%+33%
-50%+100%
-75%+300%

Losses hurt more than equivalent gains help — a halved account must double just to get back to even. This asymmetry is the mathematical argument for everything in this module: it is far easier to avoid deep drawdowns than to climb out of them.

📜 The Rules, All Together
  1. Never risk more than 1% of the account on one trade.
  2. Every trade has a stop loss placed at entry. No exceptions, no widening.
  3. Position size is calculated from the formula — never from feel.
  4. Target a minimum 1:2 risk:reward on every setup.
  5. Cap total simultaneous exposure (e.g. max 3 open trades / 3% total risk).
  6. After 3 consecutive losses, stop for the day. Streak-chasing is how tilt becomes a blown account (Module 9).

MODULE 06

🕯️ Technical Analysis Basics

Reading price charts — candlesticks, support and resistance, and trends. Useful as a framework for structuring trades; not a crystal ball, and this module is explicit about the difference.

🕯️ Candlesticks — What One Candle Tells You
high high │ │ ┌──┴──┐ ┌──┴──┐ │ │ bullish │ │ bearish │close│ (green) │open │ (red) │ │ close > open │ │ close < open │open │ │close│ └──┬──┘ └──┬──┘ │ │ low low

Each candle summarizes one time period (1 minute, 1 hour, 1 day — your chosen timeframe): where price opened, closed, and the highest/lowest it reached (the "wicks"). Long wicks show rejection — price went there and got pushed back.

🧱 Support & Resistance

Support is a price area where falling price has repeatedly stopped and bounced; resistance is where rising price has repeatedly stalled. They matter because enough traders watch the same levels that reactions become partially self-fulfilling — and they give you logical, structural places for stop losses (just beyond the level) rather than arbitrary distances.

💡
Zones, not linesSupport/resistance are areas a few pips wide, not exact prices. Treating them as precise lines leads to stops placed one pip beyond a level — exactly where they're most likely to get clipped before price reverses.
📈 Trends & Structure
  • Uptrend — successively higher highs and higher lows.
  • Downtrend — successively lower highs and lower lows.
  • Range — price oscillating between horizontal support and resistance.

"Trade with the trend" survives as advice because trading against a strong trend requires precisely timing a reversal — the single hardest thing in markets. Trend-following at least puts the prevailing flow behind you.

⚖️ What TA Is — and Isn't

Honest framing: academic evidence on technical analysis is mixed at best. Its real, defensible value for a disciplined trader isn't prediction — it's structure: consistent, pre-defined places to enter, to put stops, and to take profit, which makes the risk math of Modules 4–5 executable. A trader with mediocre analysis and excellent risk management outlasts a brilliant analyst with none, every time.

MODULE 07

📉 Indicators & Their Limits

Moving averages, RSI, and MACD — what each actually computes, what it's useful for, and the trap of stacking five of them and calling it a strategy.

📏 Moving Averages (MA)

A moving average smooths price by averaging the last N candles — a 50-period MA averages the last 50 closes. SMA weights all equally; EMA weights recent prices more heavily, so it reacts faster.

  • Trend filter — price above a rising 200 MA is the classic "uptrend" definition.
  • Crossovers — a fast MA crossing above a slow one (e.g. 50 over 200, the "golden cross") is a common trend-change signal. It's inherently late — averages lag by construction.
💪 RSI — Relative Strength Index

RSI (0–100) measures the speed of recent gains vs. losses. Traditional reading: above 70 = "overbought", below 30 = "oversold".

⚠️
The classic RSI trapIn a strong trend, RSI can sit "overbought" for weeks while price keeps climbing. Overbought does not mean "about to fall" — it means "has been rising fast." Selling something just because RSI hit 70 is one of the most common beginner losses.
🌊 MACD

MACD plots the gap between two EMAs (typically 12 and 26) plus a signal line — essentially a trend-momentum gauge. Signal-line crossovers and divergence (price making new highs while MACD doesn't) are its common uses. Like all MA-derived tools, it lags.

🚫 The Indicator Trap

Every indicator is computed from the same price data. Stacking five indicators doesn't add five independent confirmations — it adds four echoes. More indicators mostly means more conflicting signals and more reasons to rationalize a bad trade.

💡
A sane starting toolkitOne trend filter (e.g. 200 EMA), one momentum gauge (e.g. RSI), clean support/resistance — and the discipline from Module 5. If a setup needs six indicators to justify itself, it isn't a setup.

MODULE 08

📰 Fundamental Analysis

The forces that actually move currencies and stocks — interest rates, economic data, earnings — and the economic calendar every trader checks before placing anything.

🏦 Interest Rates — The Biggest Force in Forex

Currencies broadly follow interest-rate expectations. Higher rates make holding a currency more rewarding, attracting capital. That's why central bank decisions — the Fed (USD), ECB (EUR), BoE (GBP), BoJ (JPY) — are the most market-moving scheduled events that exist, and why markets move on expectations shifting, not just the decision itself.

📅 The Economic Calendar
ReleaseWhat It MeasuresTypical Impact
Central bank rate decisionsThe price of money itselfExtreme
NFP (US Non-Farm Payrolls)US job creation, first Friday monthlyExtreme, famous for violent minutes
CPI (inflation)Price growth — drives rate expectationsHigh
GDPOverall economic growthModerate–high
PMI surveysBusiness activity/sentimentModerate
⚠️
Beginners should not trade through red-calendar eventsSpreads widen, slippage explodes, and price can gap straight through stop losses. Check a free economic calendar before every session; if a high-impact release is due on your pair, standing aside is the professional move.
🏢 Fundamentals for Stocks
  • Earnings reports (quarterly) — the stock market's equivalent of NFP; single stocks routinely gap 5–15% overnight on results.
  • P/E ratio — price relative to earnings; a rough "how expensive is this company" gauge, most meaningful vs. its sector peers.
  • Guidance — management's own forecast, which often moves the stock more than the reported quarter itself.
🤝 How Traders Actually Combine the Two

A common, sane division of labor: fundamentals for direction bias ("rate expectations favor USD strength this month"), technicals for execution (where exactly to enter, where the stop goes). Neither predicts; together they give a trade a reasoned thesis and a defined structure — which is all a good trade ever has.

MODULE 09

🧠 Trading Psychology

The uncomfortable truth: most blown accounts aren't analysis failures — they're discipline failures. The patterns below are so universal they have names.

☠️ The Four Classic Account-Killers
PatternWhat It Looks LikeThe Defense
Revenge tradingImmediately re-entering after a loss to "win it back", usually biggerThe 3-losses-stop-for-the-day rule (Module 5)
Moving the stopWidening a stop loss as price approaches it — "it'll turn around"SL is placed at entry and never widened. Ever.
FOMO entriesChasing a move that already happened because "everyone's making money"No setup from your written plan = no trade
Oversizing after winsTripling size because you feel invincible after a streakPosition size comes from the formula, not the mood
🎲 Thinking in Probabilities

The single biggest mental shift: a good trade is one that followed your rules, regardless of outcome. A rule-following trade that loses was still a good trade; a reckless trade that wins was still a bad one — the market just paid you to reinforce a habit that will eventually destroy you. Any individual outcome is mostly noise; only the process compounds.

🧊 Practical Discipline Mechanics
  1. Pre-commit everything — entry, SL, TP, size — before the position is open, while nothing is at stake emotionally.
  2. Daily loss limit — e.g. down 3% on the day, platform closed. Written, not vibes.
  3. The journal is the mirror (Module 10) — most people can't see their own tilt in real time, but it's unmissable in a written record two weeks later.
  4. Size down when emotional — if you notice yourself hoping rather than assessing, you're oversized by definition.

MODULE 10

📔 Your Trading Plan & Journal

A trading plan turns everything so far into a written, checkable document — and the journal is how you find out whether you're actually following it.

📜 What a Written Plan Contains
  1. Markets & sessions — e.g. "EUR/USD and GBP/USD only, London session only."
  2. Setup definition — the exact, objective conditions that constitute a trade. If two people reading it would disagree on whether a setup exists, it isn't specific enough.
  3. Risk rules — 1% per trade, max 3% total exposure, daily loss limit, the losing-streak stop.
  4. Exit rules — where SL and TP go, and the minimum 1:2 R:R filter.
  5. Review schedule — when you audit the journal (weekly) and when the plan itself may be revised (monthly at most — never mid-losing-streak).
📓 The Journal — Every Trade, Every Field
FieldWhy It Matters
Date, pair, direction, sizeThe basics for any later statistics
Entry, SL, TP, planned R:RWas the structure right at entry?
Reason for entry (one sentence)"It looked like it would go up" written down is self-diagnosing
Followed plan? (yes/no)The single most predictive field in the whole journal
Emotional state (one word)Patterns like "bored" or "angry" preceding losses become visible fast
Outcome in R (not dollars)+2R / -1R normalizes results across position sizes
💡
The two-week revelationNearly everyone who journals honestly discovers the same thing: their rule-following trades perform fine, and nearly all their losses cluster in the "didn't follow plan" rows. That discovery — made with your own data — is worth more than any strategy video.
🧪 Paper Trade Before Real Money — With Graduation Criteria

Practice on the simulator (Module 12) exactly as if it were real. Vague practice proves nothing, so set explicit graduation criteria before going live with any real money:

  1. Minimum 50 journaled paper trades following the written plan.
  2. Plan-compliance above 90% (the yes/no field).
  3. Not down more than 10% over the full sample.
  4. Then, if ever going live: the smallest possible real size (micro lots), treating the first months as paid tuition.

🚨 Brokers, Regulation & Scams

Trading education attracts more predators than almost any other niche online. This module is your immune system.

🏦 Choosing a Broker — Regulation Is Everything

A broker holds your money. The only meaningful safety signal is regulation by a serious authority:

RegulatorRegion
FCAUnited Kingdom
CFTC / NFAUnited States
ASICAustralia
CySECEU (Cyprus — common for EU brokers)
⚠️
Verify on the regulator's own siteScam brokers claim licenses constantly. Every real regulator has a public register — search the broker's name there directly, not on the broker's own site. An offshore-only "license" (or none) means your deposit has no protection whatsoever.

Also understand the incentive structure: many retail forex brokers profit from spreads either way, and some internalize your trades — meaning your loss can literally be their gain. Regulation is what keeps that conflict of interest policed.

🎣 The Scam Field Guide

💡
